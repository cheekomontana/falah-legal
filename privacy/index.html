Privacy Policy

Falah — Never Miss Salah
Last updated: 16 August 2026

Falah is built so that almost nothing about you ever leaves your iPhone. Your prayers, your location, your answers and the apps you choose to lock are stored on your device and are not sent to us. We do not run a server that holds your data, and we do not sell anything about you to anyone.

This policy explains exactly what is stored, the few things that do leave your device, and who receives them. It covers the Falah iOS app and nothing else.

The short version

  • There is no Falah server holding your information. Signing in uses Apple's own system, and what it returns is kept on your device.
  • Your location is used on your device to calculate prayer times. We never receive it.
  • Falah cannot see which apps you lock, and cannot see your app usage. Apple's design makes that technically impossible — see below.
  • Nothing you type into the Companion leaves your phone.
  • We do not use advertising, analytics SDKs, or cross-app tracking.
  • Two companies receive limited information: Apple, and our paywall provider Superwall. Both are described below.
  • Deleting the app deletes everything Falah has stored.

1. What Falah stores on your device

All of the following is written to your iPhone's local app storage. It stays on the device, is included in your encrypted iPhone backup if you make one, and is never transmitted to us.

  • Your first name, if you choose to enter one during setup. It is used to greet you on the Home screen.
  • Your setup answers — how consistently you currently pray, which prayers you tend to miss, what usually delays you, how you feel about missing a prayer, your goal, and how strongly you want to be held accountable. These shape the plan Falah shows you.
  • Your approximate location — latitude and longitude, a place name such as "Waterloo, ON", and a two-letter country code. This is required to calculate prayer times and to pick a sensible default calculation convention for your region.
  • Your calculation settings — the calculation method and madhab you follow.
  • Your prayer log — which prayers you have confirmed, when you confirmed them, and whether each was early, on time, delayed, made up later, or missed.
  • Your Prayer Lock settings — which of the five prayers lock your apps, any scheduled locks you have created, and which individual lock windows you ended by hand.
  • Your notification preference and the date you first opened the app.
  • Your Sign in with Apple details, if you sign in — see section 2.

Some of this — your prayer habits and your religious practice — is genuinely personal. That is precisely why it is kept on your device and not on ours.

2. Signing in with Apple

Falah offers Sign in with Apple. It is Apple's own sign-in system, and it is the only sign-in method the app supports — there is no password to create and no email and password form.

When you sign in, Apple asks you whether to share your real email address or to hide it behind a private relay address that forwards to you. That choice is yours and it is made on Apple's screen, not ours. Apple then gives Falah:

  • a stable identifier for you, which is unique to Falah and cannot be used to recognise you in any other company's app;
  • your name and email address as you chose to share them — provided once, the first time you sign in.

Falah stores this on your device, in the same local storage as everything else in section 1. There is no Falah server for it to be sent to, and we do not transmit it anywhere.

You can stop sharing at any time in iOS Settings → your name → Sign in with Apple → Falah, which revokes the app's access from Apple's side. Deleting the app removes the stored credential from your device. Apple's own handling of the sign-in is governed by Apple's Privacy Policy.

3. What Falah can never see: your locked apps

This deserves its own section, because it is the most common question about an app like this.

Prayer Lock is built on Apple's Screen Time (Family Controls) framework. When you choose which apps to lock, that choice is made inside a screen drawn by iOS itself, not by Falah. Apple hands Falah an opaque token for each app — a meaningless identifier that Apple deliberately designed so that it cannot be turned back into an app name.

The practical consequences are absolute:

  • Falah does not know which apps you selected.
  • Falah cannot see how long you spend in any app, or when you open one.
  • Falah cannot see your browsing history, your messages, or your notifications.
  • Falah cannot read the contents of any app it locks.

Where the app appears to show you the icons of the apps you picked, those icons are drawn by iOS inside its own process — Falah is given a sealed view to place on screen, not the identity of what is inside it. Your selection is stored on your device and in a private container shared only between Falah and its own iOS extensions. It is never transmitted.

4. What leaves your device

Three things, and no others.

Apple — turning coordinates into a place name

When Falah works out that you are in, say, Waterloo rather than Toronto, it asks iOS to convert your coordinates into a place name. That request is handled by Apple and may be sent to Apple's servers. Falah receives only the resulting name and country code. This is a convenience for the Settings screen; if it fails, your prayer times are unaffected. Apple's handling of that request is governed by Apple's Privacy Policy.

The prayer time calculation itself happens entirely on your device using standard astronomical formulas. There is no prayer-time server and no city database — Falah works with no internet connection at all.

Apple — subscriptions

If you purchase a Falah subscription, the transaction is handled entirely by Apple through the App Store. We never see your name, your card, or your billing address. We receive only Apple's confirmation of whether a valid subscription exists.

Superwall — paywall and subscription infrastructure

Falah uses Superwall to display its subscription screen and to keep track of subscription status. The Superwall SDK is part of the app and communicates with Superwall's servers.

Superwall receives technical and commercial information, which as declared in their App Store privacy manifest includes purchase history, together with standard device and app information such as your device model, operating system version, app version, locale, and an anonymous identifier generated for your installation. It also records paywall events — that a subscription screen was shown, dismissed, or converted.

Superwall does not receive your name, your location, your prayer log, your setup answers, or anything about which apps you lock. Superwall's manifest declares that this data is not linked to your identity and is not used for tracking. Their handling of it is governed by the Superwall Privacy Policy.

5. What Falah does not do

  • No passwords, and no sign-up form. The only way to sign in is Apple's own, and it is handled on Apple's screen. We never see or store a password.
  • No advertising. Falah contains no ads and no advertising SDKs.
  • No tracking. Falah does not track you across other companies' apps or websites, and does not request App Tracking Transparency permission because it has nothing to ask for.
  • No analytics SDK. There is no Google Analytics, Firebase, Facebook SDK, Amplitude, Mixpanel, or similar in the app.
  • No selling or sharing. We do not sell, rent, or share personal information with third parties for their own marketing purposes. We never have and we do not intend to.
  • No push server. Every notification Falah sends — the adhan alert and the closing-window warning — is scheduled by your own iPhone. The app is not provisioned to receive remote push notifications at all.

6. The Companion

The Companion is the chat tab dedicated to questions about Salah. In the current version of Falah, its answers are written into the app itself and selected on your device. Nothing you type into the Companion is transmitted anywhere, it is not stored between launches, and no one — including us — can read it.

If a future version of Falah introduces a Companion that sends your questions to a server in order to answer them, that will be a material change, and this policy will be updated and the change made clear in the app before it takes effect.

7. Location permission

Falah asks for location access while using the app only. It never requests background or "always" location access. You may decline, and you may revoke access at any time in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services → Falah. Falah checks your location when it needs to establish or refresh your place; it does not follow you around.

Location accuracy is deliberately requested at a low, coarse level. Prayer times do not change meaningfully across a city, so Falah does not need to know precisely where in it you are.

8. How long data is kept, and how to delete it

Everything described in section 1 stays on your device until you remove it. There is no retention period because there is no server retaining anything.

To delete it:

  • Delete the app. iOS removes Falah's entire storage container with it, including your prayer log, your setup answers, your location and your lock settings. This is complete and irreversible.
  • Revoke Screen Time access in iOS Settings to end Falah's ability to restrict apps.
  • Revoke Sign in with Apple in iOS Settings → your name → Sign in with Apple → Falah, if you signed in.

Data held by Apple or Superwall is subject to their own policies and retention periods; requests concerning it should be directed to them, though we will help where we can — write to us at the address below.

9. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights under laws such as the GDPR (Europe and the UK), PIPEDA (Canada), or the CCPA/CPRA (California) to access, correct, delete, or port your personal information, and to object to its processing.

Because Falah holds your information on your own device and not on our servers, you already exercise most of these rights directly and without asking us: the data is in your possession, you can see it in the app, and you can erase all of it by deleting the app. We hold no copy to hand over, correct, or delete on your behalf.

Where we do act as a controller of information — principally the limited subscription information handled through Superwall and Apple — you may contact us at the address below and we will respond as the applicable law requires. We do not discriminate against anyone for exercising these rights. We do not sell personal information as those laws define the term.

For users in the European Economic Area and the UK, our lawful basis for processing the limited data described in section 3 is our legitimate interest in operating and supporting the app and in processing subscriptions, and where required, your consent — which you give by granting the relevant iOS permission and may withdraw at any time in iOS Settings.

10. Children

Falah is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. The app has no account system, no chat with other people, and no user-generated content that could be shared. If you believe a child has provided us with personal information, contact us and we will act on it.

11. Security

Your Falah data is protected by iOS itself. It is stored inside the app's private container, which other apps on your iPhone cannot read, and it is protected by your device passcode and by iOS file encryption. If you back up your iPhone, that backup is protected by Apple according to your iCloud or local backup settings.

No system is perfectly secure, but keeping data on your device rather than on a server we operate removes an entire category of risk: there is no Falah database to breach.

12. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the "Last updated" date at the top will change with it. If a change is material — particularly anything that would cause information to leave your device that previously did not — we will make that clear within the app rather than relying on you to re-read this page.
